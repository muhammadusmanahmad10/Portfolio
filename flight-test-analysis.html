Flight-Test Analysis & Radio-vs-Attitude Behaviour

On every ATG flight-test the diagnostic question is the same: why did the radio do what it did at that moment? My approach is to walk a strict diagnostic chain — RSRP → SINR/RSRQ → CQI → MCS/Rank/PMI → BLER → throughput — and overlay it against aircraft altitude, roll and bank so each anomaly can be classified: coverage, interference, receiver, link-adaptation, mobility or transport.

The diagnostic chain — what each metric actually tells me

  • RSRP — per-RE reference-signal power. On a single-frequency network this often doesn’t move much because many cells are visible at similar range — so RSRP alone is weak as a serving-cell discriminator.
  • RSSI — total band power (serving + neighbours + noise). Rising RSSI with flat RSRP is the fingerprint of rising interference.
  • RSRQ = N · RSRP / RSSI — the ratio captures how much of the band is “ours” vs other cells + noise.
  • SINR — the operating quantity on ATG. Wideband SINR tells the overall story; sub-band SINR reveals selective fading / per-RB interference.
  • CQI — UE’s request for MCS, derived from SINR estimates and target BLER. Lags SINR by several TTIs; recovers slowly after sudden changes.
  • MCS & Rank & PMI — the eNB’s chosen modulation, number of layers and precoder. Rank-1 under equal-RSRP suggests receiver or correlation problems; rank-2 requires enough SINR on both branches.
  • BLER — residual errors after HARQ. Sustained BLER with healthy SINR points above the radio (OLLA / scheduler / DCI issues).
  • Throughput — the ceiling imposed by MCS × Rank × allocated RBs minus HARQ overhead, minus whatever transport adds on top.

Flight phases — how each metric moves

  • Climb. Elevation angle to the serving cell improves then flattens; RSRP may rise briefly then stabilize; neighbour cells enter and leave the measurement set quickly — the serving-cell roster churns.
  • Cruise. Stable geometry at ~35,000 ft; RSRP mostly flat; SINR dominates the variance because the interference picture shifts as the aircraft tracks along the ground-site string.
  • Banking. Roll angles of 15–30° rotate the fuselage antennas by that amount; the V and H polarization branches swap tilt relative to the serving cell, producing transient 3–10 dB fades on one branch — often visible as a rank-2 → rank-1 drop with a brief MCS collapse.
  • Cell-edge. Dominance goes away as the UE sits between cells at near-equal RSRP — where SINR falls, CQI/MCS follow, and (if TTT is too long) handover arrives too late to escape RLF.
  • Descent. Mirror of climb, with the added risk of SIB-acquisition failures on a congested target as the UE reaches terminal altitudes.

Transient fades from roll / bank — what the data looks like

During a sustained bank, the aircraft antenna array rotates relative to the serving cell. Two things happen on the trace:

  • Branch-asymmetric RSRP. One branch drops 3–10 dB while the other stays steady — classic polarization rotation / antenna-pattern fingerprint.
  • Rank collapse. MIMO rank-2 drops to rank-1 because one layer is no longer viable; throughput roughly halves for the duration.
  • CQI/MCS lag. CQI takes a second or two to respond, so throughput sometimes recovers after SINR has already stabilized post-bank.

Takeaway: a short, branch-asymmetric SINR dip that aligns with a bank angle in the attitude trace is not a network fault — it’s geometry. Don’t chase it.

Wideband vs sub-band SINR

ATG’s narrowband DL (~9 RB) still exhibits frequency-selective behaviour. Reporting wideband SINR hides sub-band variation that matters for scheduler decisions.

  • Wideband SINR high but MCS low is a signature of selective sub-band interference — a neighbour cell punching through on specific RBs.
  • Sub-band CQI should match the scheduler’s allocation strategy. When it doesn’t, the scheduler is leaving throughput on the table.

Reading a full-flight MATLAB plot set

Per flight I generate a standardized plot set via the MATLAB scripts (see MATLAB Flight-Test Analysis Scripts):

  • Altitude + roll + bank over time.
  • Serving-cell / PCI timeline (stepped).
  • RSRP, RSRQ, RSSI, SINR per branch (V and H).
  • CQI, MCS, rank/layers per codeword.
  • BLER UL / DL.
  • DL / UL throughput, overlaid with scheduled RBs.
  • Mobility events (A3 trigger, HO command, HO complete, re-establishment, RLF) as markers.

Reading order: attitude → serving-cell → RSRP/SINR → CQI/MCS/rank → BLER → throughput → mobility events. Any claim written on an executive summary comes from this sequence.

Pitfalls I stay aware of

  • Chasing a bank-angle transient as a network fault. Always overlay attitude first.
  • Reading wideband SINR as complete. Always check sub-band for selective interference.
  • Blaming the radio for scheduler behaviour. If SINR is stable and throughput is erratic, the fault is above the radio.
  • Letting CQI lag mislead the report. After a handover, allow several seconds before concluding the post-HO MCS is “too low”.
