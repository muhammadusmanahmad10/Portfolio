5G NR PHY & Scheduling

The 5G air interface is OFDM with flexible numerology, beam-based access, and a scheduler that hands out resources slot-by-slot. This page covers the physical layer (frame structure, channels, reference signals, beam management) and the MAC scheduling, HARQ and link-adaptation behaviour that sit on top — the parameters that determine real throughput and reliability.

Beam management (FR2 & FR1 Massive MIMO)

5G is beam-based: the gNB sweeps multiple SSB beams for cell access, then refines to user-specific beams via CSI-RS reports. The lifecycle is:

  1. P1 — Initial beam acquisition over SSB sweep (UE measures SSB RSRP, picks best beam).
  2. P2 — gNB-side beam refinement using CSI-RS sweep.
  3. P3 — UE-side beam refinement.
  4. Beam-failure detection & recovery (BFD/BFR) — UE declares beam failure on consecutive bad-quality measurements and recovers via a new beam through PRACH.

This connects directly to my Massive MIMO work — beamforming gains depend on CSI feedback quality and the right beam-management configuration.

MAC scheduling, link adaptation & HARQ

ConceptWhat it does
Dynamic schedulingThe gNB scheduler hands out PDSCH/PUSCH resources slot-by-slot via PDCCH (DCI), based on buffer status, channel quality, QoS and fairness.
Semi-Persistent Scheduling (SPS) / Configured GrantPre-configured periodic DL/UL grants for predictable traffic (e.g. VoNR, URLLC) — reduces PDCCH overhead and latency.
BSR / SRBuffer Status Report & Scheduling Request — how the UE asks for UL resources.
Link adaptation (MCS, CQI, RI, PMI)UE reports CQI (channel quality), RI (rank), PMI (precoder); the gNB picks an MCS (modulation + coding rate) targeting a BLER (typically ~10%).
HARQStop-and-Wait per HARQ process; UE reports ACK/NACK on PUCCH/PUSCH; the gNB retransmits with incremental-redundancy and the UE soft-combines at PHY.
DRXDiscontinuous reception — UE sleeps for power saving; the scheduler honours active times.
Carrier Aggregation (CA)Combines multiple component carriers (PCell + SCells) for higher peak throughput.

How I use PHY/scheduling in validation

When throughput, latency or coverage misbehave, the PHY/scheduling layer often holds the real story:

  • Throughput plateau: check reported CQI/MCS — a clamped MCS points to RF or scheduler config, not transport.
  • High latency under load: check HARQ retx rate and DRX configuration; PDCCH congestion can starve users.
  • Coverage edge: SSB RSRP & beam-failure events; RF optimization (tilt/power) then closes the gap.
  • VoNR call quality: SPS / configured-grant config and 5QI 1 mapping; voice page.
