PCI Planning for 2-TX ATG Cells — mod-3 / mod-6 / Frame Offset

On the single-frequency ATG network, PCI planning isn’t just about mod-6 CRS collisions — on 2-TX (2T2R) cells, which CRS subcarriers are actually occupied is governed by PCI mod 3, and PSS follows the same mod-3 pattern. Two cells with the same mod-3 collide on both CRS and PSS, corrupting channel estimation and sync for both. I identified that the ATG 2T2R cells had been planned on mod-6 alone and drove a PCI re-plan to remove mod-3 conflicts — measurably improving SINR and cutting cell-edge RLF / re-establishment failures.

PCI mod 6 (CRS shift) PCI mod 3 (CRS REs on 2-TX) PSS = PCI mod 3 Frame offset SINR improvement

Three independent mod relationships

  • PCI mod 6 — CRS frequency shift. Determines which of the six possible frequency positions the CRS (Cell-specific Reference Signal) sits at. Two cells with the same mod-6 overlap their CRS REs at all antenna-port configurations.
  • PCI mod 3 — CRS REs occupied on 2-TX cells, and PSS. On 2-TX cells only two of the three CRS positions in each block are populated (ports 0 and 1). Which two are populated is set by PCI mod 3. PSS uses one of three Zadoff–Chu sequences selected by NID(2) = PCI mod 3. Two cells with the same mod-3 collide on CRS and on PSS, corrupting channel estimation and initial sync for both.
  • Frame offset / sub-frame offset. Governs the time alignment of each cell’s radio frame. Offsetting frames between co-channel cells moves CRS collisions in time, which can partially mitigate mod-6 overlap — but doesn’t fix mod-3 because PSS is transmitted on fixed slots regardless.

Why mod-3, not just mod-6, drives the ATG problem

Terrestrial 4-TX LTE deployments typically plan on PCI mod 6 (all six CRS positions in play). ATG is 2-TX (2T2R) — only two CRS REs out of the three in each block are populated, and PCI mod 3 picks which two. The cascading consequences:

  • Two cells with the same mod-3 put CRS on the same REs, destroying each other’s channel estimates whenever both cells are visible.
  • PSS from both cells lands on the same Zadoff–Chu sequence, confusing the UE’s initial cell-search and PCI detection logic.
  • SIB-acquisition failures become more likely on mod-3-conflicted neighbours because the UE must decode cell-specific channels while neighbour CRS is corrupting its estimates.

On a mod-6 plan alone, two cells can share mod-3 (e.g. PCI 0 and PCI 3 both have mod 3 = 0) and look “different” by mod-6 while still colliding on mod-3.

When the damage appears (and why it’s hidden until near-equal RSRP)

A mod-3 conflict is only visible when the two cells arrive at the UE at near-equal RSRP. If one cell is strongly dominant, the weaker one is below the noise floor on CRS/PSS REs and doesn’t corrupt anything. This is why mod-3 damage:

  • Only shows up at cell-edge (or on an SFN where dominance is naturally weak).
  • Correlates with a specific ground-geometry (where two conflicted cells have similar range).
  • Can be invisible on dominant-coverage drive tests and only emerges in flight-test data where the aircraft sits between cells for extended periods.

Case — PCI re-plan that measurably improved SINR

Finding
The 2T2R ATG cells had been planned without accounting for PCI mod-3. Several adjacent cells shared mod-3 — PCI 0/3, 1/4, 2/5, etc. — so CRS and PSS collided at cell-edge.
Evidence
Flight-test logs showed SINR depression at specific geometric locations that mapped to pairs of mod-3-conflicted cells; additional RLFs with re-establishment-failure (T311) and SIB-acquisition failures on the mod-3-conflicted neighbour.
Recommendation & validation
Re-plan PCIs so that each cell’s mod-3 differs from its first-tier neighbours (ideally from the second tier too). Where re-planning the whole region was not feasible, use frame-offset staggering to at least move CRS time-domain collisions — though frame offset alone doesn’t fix PSS.
Outcome
SINR improved measurably at the previously-problematic cell-edge geometries, and cell-edge RLF / re-establishment-failure counts dropped on subsequent flight campaigns.

Takeaway: on 2-TX cells, PCI planning must respect mod-3 (not just mod-6) — especially on a single-frequency network where every neighbour is co-channel and dominance cannot be assumed.

Frame offset — what it does and doesn’t fix

  • Offsetting frames between co-channel cells shifts CRS collisions in time: a conflicted RE now lands on a different subframe for one of the cells.
  • Helps with mod-6 CRS collisions and mod-3 CRS on PDSCH timing — mitigation, not cure.
  • Does not fix PSS collisions — PSS is transmitted on fixed symbols (subframes 0 and 5) and uses NID(2) = PCI mod 3. Two cells with the same mod-3 sit on the same PSS sequence regardless of frame offset.
  • Use frame offset as a mitigation when a full PCI re-plan is impractical, but address mod-3 as the primary fix wherever feasible.

Pitfalls I stay aware of

  • Planning by mod-6 alone on 2-TX cells — misses the mod-3 collision entirely.
  • Testing only in the dominant-coverage lab setup — the problem hides until the UE sees two conflicted cells at near-equal RSRP.
  • Assuming frame-offset is a full substitute for PCI re-plan — it’s partial, PSS is still colliding.
  • Chasing the resulting SINR depression as interference from a far-off third cell — always check the near-equal-RSRP neighbour’s mod-3 first.
