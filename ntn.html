Non-Terrestrial Networks (NTN)

NTN extends 5G beyond cell towers — using satellites and high-altitude platforms to deliver coverage where terrestrial sites can't reach. Standardised from 3GPP Release 17, it's one of the fastest-growing frontiers in RAN. I've worked on mixed terrestrial/satellite-linked sites and the coexistence questions NTN raises — a differentiator few RAN engineers have hands-on exposure to.

Where NTN fits

NTN inserts a satellite (transparent or regenerative payload) into the path between the UE and the 5G core via service and feeder links — extending coverage to remote, maritime and disaster-recovery scenarios where building towers isn't feasible.

My exposure

On a large smart-city telecom infrastructure program I integrated 5G and RAN-sharing (MORAN) solutions across sites whose backhaul transport was carried over satellite (i.e. terrestrial 5G RAN with satellite-based backhaul, not 3GPP NTN air-interface). The terrestrial-vs-satellite spectrum coexistence intuition that work built — PFD (power flux density) limits and adjacent-band coexistence rules — carries directly into NTN, where the air interface itself uses satellite.

What makes NTN hard (and where validation focuses)

ChallengeWhy it matters
Long propagation delaySatellite distance adds large round-trip delay — timers, HARQ, and RACH must be adapted (Rel-17 NTN timing enhancements).
Doppler shiftFast-moving LEO satellites shift frequency — needs pre-compensation.
PFD limits & coexistenceSatellite emissions must stay within power-flux-density limits to coexist with terrestrial networks in shared bands.
Mobility & cell movementFor LEO, the "cell" moves over the ground — fundamentally different mobility model from fixed towers.

Field notes

  • NTN reuses the 5G NR stack with timing/Doppler adaptations — not a separate radio technology.
  • Coexistence is the gating issue in shared spectrum; PFD limits are the terrestrial network's protection.
  • Delay budget dominates design — procedures tuned for terrestrial latency must be re-checked for NTN.
