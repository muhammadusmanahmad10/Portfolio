UL MIMO on ATG — DCI Format 4, Rank & Codewords

ATG uplink is 2T aircard → 2R eNB, so UL spatial multiplexing is on the table. Reading the uplink trace cleanly requires separating codewords from layers, understanding the per-codeword MCS gap, and knowing when a high cw1 BLER at low MCS is a healthy “probing” behaviour rather than a fault.

Codewords vs layers — the one place to not get sloppy

  • Codeword (CW) — an independently-coded transport block with its own MCS and HARQ process.
  • Layer — a spatial stream carried on the air. A codeword is mapped to one or more layers.
  • Common UL configurations on 2x2:
    • 1C1L (1 codeword, 1 layer) — rank-1; single codeword mapped to one layer. The baseline.
    • 2C2L (2 codewords, 2 layers) — rank-2 spatial mux; each CW independently coded and mapped to its own layer. Doubles peak UL throughput when SINR and channel conditioning allow.
  • cw0 and cw1 — the two independently-coded transport blocks in 2C2L. Each has its own MCS, its own HARQ ACK/NACK, its own BLER.

DCI format 4 — the UL spatial-multiplex grant

DCI format 4 is the LTE UL grant that enables UL spatial multiplexing. Key fields to read in the trace:

  • RB allocation for the PUSCH.
  • MCS and NDI per codeword — two sets.
  • Precoding information (TPMI) — which precoder the eNB wants the UE to apply.
  • Number of layers — implied by the CW-to-layer mapping and TPMI.
  • TPC command for PUSCH power.

1C1L grants use the simpler UL DCI formats (0/0A/0B); 2C2L uses format 4.

The per-codeword MCS gap

In 2C2L, the eNB rarely assigns the same MCS to both codewords. The gap exists for several reasons:

  • Spatial eigenvalues differ. The two layers see different effective SNRs after precoding.
  • OLLA per-CW adjustment. Outer-loop link adaptation adjusts each CW’s MCS independently based on its own BLER target.
  • Probing. The eNB deliberately pushes cw1 to a slightly higher MCS than its past success suggests to test whether the channel can support more — a controlled BLER on cw1 is expected.

So a trace showing cw0 clean at MCS 18 and cw1 with 20–30% BLER at MCS 10 is often the probing behaviour working as designed — not a scheduler fault.

PUSCH power split in UL spatial mux

  • Total PUSCH power is split across the transmitting antennas; the per-antenna PA headroom determines the achievable per-layer SNR at the eNB.
  • On 2C2L the UE uses both PAs — so maximum PUSCH power per antenna is lower than in 1C1L where all power goes to a single antenna.
  • Consequence: at cell-edge where power is the limit, 1C1L can actually deliver more usable throughput than 2C2L, because it concentrates energy on a single layer.
  • The scheduler should drop rank to 1 near cell-edge — if it doesn’t, you’ll see a sustained cw1 fail with very high BLER and no OLLA recovery.

Rank adaptation on a flight trace

  • Rank is a function of channel conditioning (eigenvalue spread) and SINR.
  • On ATG the common rank-collapse triggers are: bank-angle polarization rotation, a cell-edge SINR collapse, or receiver-chain asymmetry on one branch.
  • In the trace, rank is usually visible as a step signal; MCS/throughput should follow the step.
  • If rank stays at 2 while SINR is collapsing, you’ve likely got a rank-adaptation delay bug or an overly-sticky OLLA.

What a “healthy” UL trace looks like

  • cw0 BLER near the OLLA target (~10% initial, <1% residual after HARQ).
  • cw1 BLER a bit higher — typical of probing.
  • MCS per CW adjusting smoothly, not oscillating.
  • Rank stepping down cleanly at cell-edge, up cleanly when conditions return.
  • No sustained MAC-RA attempts in the trace (no UL access problem).

Pitfalls I stay aware of

  • Conflating codewords and layers. “High cw1 BLER” is not “high layer-2 BLER” — the mapping matters.
  • Reading cw1 probing BLER as a fault. It’s expected when cw0 is healthy.
  • Expecting 2C2L throughput at cell-edge. Power-limited rank-2 under-performs rank-1.
  • Missing a stuck-rank-2 scheduler bug — verify rank follows SINR, not the other way around.
