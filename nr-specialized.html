5G NR Specialized Features

ℹ️Knowledge page. Specialized NR features standardised in Rel-16/17/18 that any senior RAN engineer should be able to discuss, even if not all are in their hands-on deployment scope.

Beyond mainstream eMBB voice and data, 5G NR specifies several specialized features that broaden the technology into ultra-reliable low-latency applications, spectrum sharing with LTE, self-backhauled radio nodes, broadcast/multicast, and direct device-to-device (sidelink) communication. This page is the quick-reference on each.

URLLC — Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communications

What it is: the NR profile for use cases that need 1 ms user-plane latency and 10-5 packet error rate — industrial automation, remote control, smart-grid protection.

How NR achieves it:

  • Mini-slot scheduling — 2/4/7 OFDM symbols instead of a full 14-symbol slot, so a packet starts and finishes faster.
  • PDCP duplication over multiple RLC entities / cells, then duplicate-discard at the receiver — diversity for reliability.
  • Configured Grant (CG) / Semi-Persistent Scheduling (SPS) — pre-allocated resources, no Scheduling-Request round-trip.
  • HARQ-less or aggressive HARQ with very low BLER target (~10-5 at first transmission for the URLLC traffic class).
  • Pre-emption indication — eMBB resources can be pre-empted by URLLC traffic mid-slot.
  • Compact DCI (formats 0_2 / 1_2) for fast control decoding.

3GPP context: introduced in Rel-15, enhanced in Rel-16 (IIoT) and Rel-17.

DSS — Dynamic Spectrum Sharing

What it is: LTE and NR share the same carrier dynamically, slot-by-slot, instead of statically partitioning the spectrum. The same MHz can carry LTE one millisecond and NR the next, with the scheduler deciding the split based on traffic. The NR side typically rate-matches its PDSCH around the LTE cell-specific reference signals (CRS) and uses MBSFN-like subframes on LTE to free symbols for NR. Critical for operators introducing 5G on low/mid bands while LTE traffic still dominates — no spectrum re-farming hit. 3GPP Release 15+ feature.

IAB — Integrated Access & Backhaul

What it is: a gNB whose backhaul is the NR air interface itself instead of fibre/MW — particularly attractive in mmWave (FR2) where wired backhaul to every cell is impractical.

  • IAB-donor — the gNB with wired backhaul to the 5GC.
  • IAB-node — gNB-like node that backhauls itself over NR to the IAB-donor (or another IAB-node, multi-hop).
  • BH RLC channels + BAP (Backhaul Adaptation Protocol) route traffic across IAB hops.

3GPP context: Rel-16 (initial), Rel-17 (mobile IAB, enhancements).

NR MBS — Multicast / Broadcast Services

What it is: 5G NR's standardised point-to-multipoint delivery — one transmission serves many UEs in a cell. Replaces LTE's eMBMS. Two modes: Multicast (group of known UEs, with RRC context and feedback) and Broadcast (no UE-specific context, anyone in the cell can decode). Use cases include public-safety alerts, software updates pushed to many devices, live event streaming, V2X group safety messages. Specified in Rel-17 (TS 23.247, 38.300).

Sidelink (PC5) / NR V2X

What it is: direct UE-to-UE communication over the PC5 reference point, without traffic having to go through the gNB. The 5G evolution of LTE-V (Rel-14) into NR V2X for vehicle-to-everything (vehicle-to-vehicle, vehicle-to-infrastructure, vehicle-to-pedestrian).

  • Mode 1 — gNB schedules sidelink resources for the UEs.
  • Mode 2 — UEs autonomously select resources (no network coverage required).
  • Unicast / groupcast / broadcast over PC5; HARQ feedback supported on PC5.
  • Beyond V2X, sidelink is being extended for commercial proximity services (ProSe) and public safety.

3GPP context: NR V2X in Rel-16; ProSe sidelink enhancements in Rel-17/18.

mMTC concepts

Massive-Machine-Type Communications — the ITU-R IMT-2020 use case for huge numbers of low-cost, low-power devices. In practice, this is covered today by NB-IoT and LTE-M (CAT-M) (both LTE-anchored, evolving alongside 5G); the NR-native equivalent is RedCap (Rel-17, reduced-capability NR for wearables / industrial sensors) and the lighter eRedCap in Rel-18. Trade-off across the family is the same: lower bandwidth, fewer Rx branches, longer battery life, simpler stack, narrower use-case.
