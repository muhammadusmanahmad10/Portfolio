Spectrum · Refarming & Coexistence

Spectrum Refarming & Coexistence

Spectrum is finite, so operators refarm it — re-purposing bands from older technologies (2G/3G/4G) to 5G as demand shifts. Done wrong, refarming creates cross-band interference and coverage holes. I integrated refarming and coexistence across the full low-to-mid band range (L700 → N77), allowing minimal interference and healthy link budgets in dense clusters.

Bands I worked across

Low bands (L700/L900, N28) carry coverage; mid bands (N41, N77) carry capacity. Refarming and coexistence decisions trade these off against each other.

What refarming actually involves

Refarming reassigns part (or all) of a band from an old RAT to a new one — e.g. narrowing a 2G/3G carrier to free spectrum for LTE or NR. The risks are coverage loss on the shrunk technology and interference between the old and new carriers at the boundary. I integrated refarming activities (including L900 and L900Cr2 work, and 2G+4G refarming) allowing the legacy service still met its coverage/quality benchmark and the new carrier came up cleanly — maintaining consistent quality through the transition.

Coexistence & Link-Budget Considerations During Integration

Coexistence

During integration of new LTE/NR carriers at multi-band sites, verified that newly activated carriers coexisted correctly with existing technologies and bands without introducing service impact. Checked for adjacent-channel interference, guard-band compliance, and inter-band interactions after on-air activation to ensure the new carrier did not degrade the performance of neighbouring LTE, NR, or legacy layers.

Link-Budget Considerations

During site integration and on-air acceptance, confirmed that configured transmit power, antenna parameters, and carrier settings aligned with the planned RF design so that expected coverage and service levels were achieved. Verified that low-band carriers provided broad coverage while mid-band NR layers delivered the intended capacity without creating coverage gaps or excessive overlap with neighbouring cells.

💡Coexistence work connects directly to 5G NR design rules (PFD limits, adjacent-band emission masks) — the same coexistence thinking carries into NTN, where satellite and terrestrial layers must share spectrum. See Non-Terrestrial Networks.

Field notes & common pitfalls

  • Refarming shrinks the legacy layer. Confirm the old RAT still meets coverage before celebrating the new carrier.
  • Guard bands aren't free. Too small ⇒ interference; too large ⇒ wasted spectrum.
  • Coverage vs capacity trade-off is band-driven. Don't expect mid-band (N41/N77) to replicate low-band (L700/N28) footprint.
  • Monitor in dense clusters, not just isolated cells — interference effects only appear under realistic geometry.
