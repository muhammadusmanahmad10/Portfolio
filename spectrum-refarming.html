Spectrum Refarming & Coexistence

Spectrum is finite, so operators refarm it — re-purposing bands from older technologies (2G/3G/4G) to 5G as demand shifts. Done wrong, refarming creates cross-band interference and coverage holes. I validated refarming and coexistence across the full low-to-mid band range (L700 → N77), confirming minimal interference and healthy link budgets in dense clusters.

Bands I worked across

Low bands (L700/L900, N28) carry coverage; mid bands (N40/N41, N77) carry capacity. Refarming and coexistence decisions trade these off against each other.

What refarming actually involves

Refarming reassigns part (or all) of a band from an old RAT to a new one — e.g. narrowing a 2G/3G carrier to free spectrum for LTE or NR. The risks are coverage loss on the shrunk technology and interference between the old and new carriers at the boundary. I validated refarming activities (including L900 and L900Cr2 work, and 4G→5G refarming) by confirming the legacy service still met its coverage/quality benchmark and the new carrier came up cleanly — maintaining consistent quality through the transition.

Coexistence & link-budget validation

Coexistence

Validated minimal cross-band interference when multiple bands share a site/cluster — checking adjacent-channel leakage, guard-band adequacy and inter-band desensitisation, so a new NR carrier doesn't degrade a neighbouring legacy band (or vice-versa).

Link budget

Optimised link budgets in high-density clusters — balancing transmit power, antenna gain, path loss and interference margin so each band's coverage matches plan. Lower bands stretch coverage; mid bands need denser sites for the same footprint.

💡Coexistence work connects directly to 5G NR design rules (PFD limits, adjacent-band emission masks) — the same coexistence thinking carries into NTN, where satellite and terrestrial layers must share spectrum. See Non-Terrestrial Networks.

Field notes & common pitfalls

  • Refarming shrinks the legacy layer. Confirm the old RAT still meets coverage before celebrating the new carrier.
  • Guard bands aren't free. Too small ⇒ interference; too large ⇒ wasted spectrum.
  • Coverage vs capacity trade-off is band-driven. Don't expect mid-band (N41/N77) to replicate low-band (L700/N28) footprint.
  • Validate in dense clusters, not just isolated cells — interference effects only appear under realistic geometry.
