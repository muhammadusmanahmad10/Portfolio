Zero-Touch Provisioning & IPsec

A 5G SA femto is plug-and-play: plug it into any internet connection and it provisions itself — builds secure tunnels, pulls its configuration, validates its physical location, and comes on air with no manual setup. That is Zero-Touch Provisioning (ZTP), and it rides on IPsec tunnels to the operator. I validate the whole bring-up, the geo-security that locks it to an approved location, and the certificate lifecycle.

ZTP bring-up sequence

On power-up the femto opens one temporary IPsec tunnel to the security gateway. The device certificate enrollment (PKI per TS 33.310 — CMP/CMPv2) is completed first inside this temporary tunnel; only after the femto holds its issued operator certificate does the OAM exchange proceed, the femto registers with the NMS, and its location is verified. Only after location is confirmed are the two permanent tunnels built — one to the NMS, one to the 5G Core. ZTP then auto-provisions cell parameters, neighbour relations, and the BMC / LMF / GMLC components; I verify the NMS correctly handles the ZTP file uploads and responds with the spectrum/BMC/LMF/GMLC pieces.

Geo-security — the 20 m location lock

Because the femto is portable, it must not work outside its authorised spot. I validated that the femto checks its position and stays in service only within a 20 m radius — move it beyond 20 m and it is automatically blocked for security. Certificate lifecycle

IPsec to the operator is certificate-authenticated, so I validate the device initial certificate enrollment (the femto obtaining its identity certificate on first bring-up) and certificate renewal (rolling the cert before expiry without dropping service). A broken enrollment or a missed renewal means no tunnel — and therefore no service — so this is a core part of turn-up validation.

Case study — IPsec/transport faults that blocked bring-up

Traffic-selector mismatch

An interface wouldn't establish because the traffic selector defined at the security gateway was 10.0.0.0 while the peer site was 42.0.0.0 — packets were dropped after the SeGW. Aligning selectors fixed reachability.

DPD timeout under load

Under high load the IPsec tunnel went down on a DPD timeout and the femto stopped sending to the SeGW — surfacing as RRC establishment failures until the tunnel recovered.

Why the plug-and-play model matters (deployment context)

This internet-delivered femto is ideal where Wi-Fi exists but VoWiFi isn't viable — e.g. enterprises where guests can't access corporate Wi-Fi, employees prefer not to join it, or the company applies DSCP marking that permits browsing but not VoWiFi. The femto delivers licensed 5G SA voice/data without depending on the enterprise Wi-Fi policy. Understanding this context is what lets me prioritise the test cases that actually matter for the deployment.

Field notes & common pitfalls

  • Location gate is a hard dependency. Permanent tunnels (and service) only come up after the ≤20 m check passes.
  • Factory reset ≠ reboot. Only factory reset clears config and re-triggers ZTP — important when reproducing field issues.
  • Certificates are silent killers. Enrollment/renewal failures look like generic "no service"; check the tunnel and cert state first.
  • Traffic selectors must match end-to-end — a pingable path can still drop the tunnel's protected traffic.

Primary references

RFC 7296 — IKEv2.  ·  3GPP TS 33.310 — network domain security / PKI (certificate enrollment, CMP).  ·  3GPP TS 33.501 — 5G security architecture.  ·  3GPP TS 28.xxx — management & provisioning (ZTP/NMS).

IPsec / IKE building blocks I validate

ElementWhat it is & what I check
IKE SA (IKESA)The control-plane Security Association negotiated by IKE_SA_INIT — it carries the keying material, DH group, encryption / integrity algorithms and a SPI pair used to protect all subsequent IKE messages. I check that IKE_SA_INIT completes (DH exchange + nonces + cookies if anti-DoS is on), that the negotiated proposals match the SeGW (encryption / PRF / integrity / DH group), and that rekey fires before the lifetime expires without dropping the IKE channel.
IKE_AUTHThe second exchange that authenticates the peers and creates the first Child SA. I verify the femto presents the correct device certificate (CN/SAN matches the SeGW expectation), the SeGW chain is validated against the trusted CA, and that the EAP / cert-only path matches the design.
Child SA (IPsec SA)The actual data-plane SA created under the IKE SA — one pair per direction, with its own SPI, keys and selectors. I verify that one Child SA covers temp / NMS / 5GC traffic as designed and that rekey (time- or byte-based) works without packet loss.
Traffic Selectors (TSi / TSr)The address / port ranges that say what traffic the Child SA protects. I match the femto's local selector to the SeGW's peer selector — the 10.0.0.0 vs 42.0.0.0 case was exactly a TS mismatch.
Encryption / Integrity proposale.g. AES-GCM-256 / AES-CBC-256 + SHA-256 / SHA-384. I verify proposal alignment between peers — one mismatched transform in the proposal kills IKE_SA_INIT.
DH group / PFSDH group used for IKE SA and for Child SA rekey (PFS). I confirm the group is enabled on both sides (e.g. group 14 / 19 / 20) and that PFS-on-rekey behaves.
DPD (Dead Peer Detection)Liveness probes over the IKE SA. I tune interval / retries and reproduce the load-induced DPD timeout case so the SA tears down cleanly and rebuilds rather than going zombie.
NAT-T (RFC 3947 / 3948)UDP-encapsulated ESP on port 4500 when a NAT is in the path. I confirm NAT-D payload detection, the keepalive cadence, and that the femto behind enterprise NAT still rekeys cleanly.
Device certificate & CA chainOperator-issued cert (TS 33.310 / CMPv2). I check enrollment on first bring-up, the chain to the operator Root CA, expiry / renewal behaviour, and that revocation (CRL / OCSP) is honoured.
SeGW reachability & FQDN / IP listThe SeGW set the femto tries (primary / backup), DNS resolution of the SeGW FQDN, and failover when the primary SeGW is unreachable.
