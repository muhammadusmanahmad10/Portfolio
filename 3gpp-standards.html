Release evolution — what each brought

I track Study Item / Work Item progress for RAN2 (radio protocols — RRC, MAC), RAN3 (interfaces & architecture — Xn/NG, mobility, RAN sharing) and RAN4 (RF & performance requirements, coexistence). My validation work maps directly onto these: mobility/SRB to RAN2, Xn/NG and AMF behaviour to RAN3, and refarming/coexistence/NTN PFD limits to RAN4.

Standards literacy in action

The clearest proof that reading the spec matters: in the SRB criticalExtension dispute, a vendor claimed a peer was non-compliant. By citing TS 38.331 (RRCReconfiguration is UE-facing; srb-ToAddModList optional when unchanged) and TS 38.423 (XnAP treats the RRC container as transparent), I demonstrated the peer was fully compliant and reframed the issue as a vendor interop alignment — closing a dispute that could otherwise have escalated for weeks.

I'm also comfortable drafting technical inputs, analysing contributions, and summarising meeting outcomes for stakeholders — translating raw spec text into decisions the team can act on.

Field notes

  • "Optional" is a precise word in 3GPP. Optional-when-unchanged IEs are a frequent source of false non-compliance claims.
  • Transparent containers mean the carrier doesn't parse them. XnAP/NGAP relay RRC without deriving its state.
  • Map the working group to the problem. RAN2 vs RAN3 vs RAN4 framing speeds up who owns a fix.
