Policy & QoS Control (PCF / PCRF)

ℹ️This is a knowledge page, not hands-on work. It documents how policy/QoS control works end-to-end so I can reason about why a 5QI/ARP or slice policy appears the way it does in my RAN traces. My hands-on work is RAN-side QoS/slicing validation (see WPS & Slicing).

QoS doesn't originate in the RAN — it's decided by policy in the core and pushed down. In 5G the PCF (and in LTE the PCRF) decides the 5QI/QCI, GBR/MBR, ARP and charging rules per flow, and the SMF/PGW enforces them. Understanding this chain explains why a bearer or QoS flow shows up with the values it does when I read it on the air interface.

5G: PCF · SMF · UPF (N7) LTE: PCRF · PCEF/PGW (Gx/Rx) 5QI/QCI · ARP · GBR/MBR

5G policy chain (PDU session)

On PDU session setup the SMF queries the PCF over N7; the PCF evaluates the subscriber profile (via UDR/UDM) and returns policy (session-AMBR, 5QI, ARP, charging). The SMF installs the rules into the UPF and signals the gNB to establish the QoS flow with the chosen 5QI/GBR/MBR. For VoNR, the P-CSCF (AF) talks to the PCF over N5, which adds a dedicated QoS flow (5QI 1) for the voice media.

5G PCF vs LTE PCRF — the mapping

ConceptLTE (PCRF)5G (PCF)
Enforcement pointPCEF in PGWSMF + UPF
Policy interfaceGx (Diameter)N7 (HTTP/2 + JSON, SBA)
App function (voice)Rx from P-CSCFN5 from P-CSCF
Subscriber dataSPR (Sp)UDR (N36) / UDM
ChargingOCS (Gy) / OFCS (Gz)CHF (N15)
Bearer modelDefault + dedicated bearersDefault + dedicated QoS flows
QoS identifierQCI5QI

5G replaces Diameter with a service-based architecture (HTTP/2 + JSON REST APIs) and replaces dedicated bearers with dedicated QoS flows — but the principle is identical: policy decides QoS & charging, enforcement applies it.

Why a RAN engineer benefits from knowing this

When I see a 5QI/ARP value, a missing dedicated flow, or a slice-specific QoS on the air interface, the root cause is often upstream policy, not the radio. Knowing the PCF/SMF chain lets me say "the RAN applied exactly what policy sent — check the PCF rule / subscriber profile" instead of chasing a non-existent RAN bug. It's the same logic as the WPS dynamic ARP-uplift and E911 policy-driven fallback behaviours I validated: the network is doing what policy told it to.

Policy use-cases

  • QoS enforcement — assign 5QI/QCI, GBR/MBR, ARP per flow.
  • Network slicing — slice-specific QoS policy per S-NSSAI.
  • Dynamic updates — modify rules mid-session (throttle, boost) via PCF→SMF (5G).
  • Zero-rating / service differentiation — DPI (Deep Packet Inspection)-assisted rules to exempt or shape specific apps.

References
