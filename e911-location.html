Emergency Services · Location & Public Warning

E911 Location & CMAS / WEA

For an emergency call, the network must report where the caller is — including altitude (Z-axis) so responders can find the right floor. 5G SA computes this with the LMF (Location Management Function) and delivers it to the GMLC, using methods like NR-ECID, GNSS and DBH. I validate that the right method is selected, that Z-axis is accurate, and that public-warning broadcasts (CMAS/WEA) reach devices reliably — all per FCC (Federal Communications Commission) and 3GPP rules.

3GPP TS 38.305 (NRPPa/positioning) 3GPP TS 23.273 (LCS) FCC E911 / Z-axis NR-ECID · GNSS · DBH Validated on a live 5G SA Femto deployment

How a 5G SA location fix is produced

The AMF requests location from the LMF; the LMF runs the positioning calculation (using gNB/UE measurements) and returns a fix with an uncertainty value. I confirmed by trace that the AMF was correctly requesting location and the femto was supplying NR-ECID measurement data — which removes the risk of ECID-based location simply not being available.

Case study — why location sometimes returned CID instead of NR-ECID

Symptom
During E911 VoNR testing, the fix sometimes came back as coarse CID (cell-level) even though the NR-ECID procedure had triggered successfully.
Investigation
The LMF was configured with a cell radius = 15 m, used as the CID location uncertainty. The LMF tries NR-ECID first, gets a calculated uncertainty back, and compares it against the CID uncertainty.
Root cause
In the lab, NR-ECID uncertainty came out slightly above 15 m. Since ECID wasn't "better" than the 15 m CID value, the LMF's quality rule rejected ECID and fell back to CID. This is a policy/threshold decision, not an RF failure — every measurement step worked.
Proof
When I raised the LMF radius above 15 m, NR-ECID became "acceptable" and was returned consistently — confirming the behaviour was threshold-driven, not a network fault.

Takeaway: "wrong" location method is often an acceptance-policy artefact. Knowing the LMF compares ECID uncertainty against the configured CID radius lets you explain — and tune — the behaviour instead of chasing a non-existent RF bug.

NR-ECID vs CID — executive view

CIDNR-ECID
BasisCell coordinate (coarse)Measurement-based (Rx-Tx, RSRP, etc.)
AccuracyCell-level fallbackMore precise when accepted
Selected whenECID uncertainty not better than CIDECID accuracy beats the CID uncertainty threshold

Net rule: NR-ECID is preferred whenever its accuracy is better than CID; otherwise the LMF safely falls back to CID.

Case study — location not reaching E911 (single-LMF dependency)

The E911 team wasn't receiving location at all during a call. The femto cell was configured at only one LMF, and that LMF was down for a software upgrade — so there was no node available to compute the fix. Resolved by provisioning the femto cell at the other three LMFs as well, removing the single point of failure.

Bonus operational save: when a GMLC/LMF-provisioned femto went down mid-campaign, I avoided missing the test slot by re-using the PCI and gNodeB ID of the whitelisted femto on a spare femto. Because NRCI is provisioned at the GMLC/LMF and TAC/PLMN were already identical, only PCI and gNB-ID differed — matching them let E911 testing proceed seamlessly.

E911 scenarios I validated

  • Location methods & Z-axis: verified DBH, GNSS and ECID-based location, with Z-axis (altitude) confirmation alongside latitude/longitude.
  • Call types & states: E911 VoNR call, RTT call, VoNR↔RTT upgrade/downgrade, across RRC inactive / idle / connected, in both SIM and SIM-less scenarios; IMS-unregistered device; rebid; SMS to E911.
  • Device restriction: the femto is 5G SA VoNR-only, so a non-VoNR device is blocked from camping on it and its E911 call falls back to the macro layer (typically lower RSRP) — validated and explained, not flagged as a fault.
  • Mobility during 911: handover scenarios femto↔femto, macro→femto→macro.
  • Mismatched-PLMN E911: verified that an E911 call from a UE on a non-matching PLMN is still routed correctly to the local PSAP (regulatory requirement — emergency calls must succeed regardless of subscription).
  • Pre-emption behaviour under congestion: verified ongoing 911 calls are not pre-empted when the cell saturates (see the PCI/PVI fix).
  • MTLR (Mobile-Terminated Location Request): a location-services procedure where the network or an external application requests the location of a mobile subscriber. Tested via EMS and VAS client.

CMAS / WEA — public warning broadcast

Coverage: validated Send / Replace / Update / Cancel, multiple alerts in sequence, across RRC inactive / idle / connected — AMBER, RMT (English & Spanish), Presidential, Public Safety, State/Local, Severe, and geo-fenced alerts, plus PWS failure and restart indications (failure verified via software restart of the femto, restart indication via hardware restart).

Case I worked: on receiving a CMAS message the femto auto-restarted due to a core-dump; the UE then stopped getting the alert (only the femto received it). The permanent fix changed the SIB message sequence-allocation parameter (the BCCH/SIB scheduling window that controls when SIB-carried Warning messages are sent on the air; the original allocation collided with the femto’s own SIB-update cycle, which is why receiving a CMAS message triggered an internal reset). Adjusting that parameter de-conflicted the SIB scheduling so the UE could receive CMAS without the femto restarting.

Field notes & common pitfalls

  • CID fallback is a feature, not a bug. Compare ECID uncertainty against the configured CID radius before declaring an RF problem.
  • Provision location at multiple LMFs. A single-LMF dependency means one maintenance window kills E911 location.
  • Z-axis is mandatory. Lat/long alone is insufficient for FCC vertical-location requirements.
  • Non-VoNR devices fall back to macro for 911 — expected, because they're barred from the VoNR-only femto.

Location-services components I work across

E911 spans the RAN, the 5G core and the emergency-services ecosystem. The pieces I provision and trace against:

ComponentRole in the location fixWhat I provision / check
UESource of GNSS / barometric / Rx-Tx / RSRP measurements; carries the LPP client and the DBH stack. Barometric measurements come from the smartphone’s barometer sensor, which measures air pressure — since air pressure varies with altitude, the network can use barometric data to estimate the user’s height or floor level inside a building (Z-axis). DBH = Device-Based Hybrid: a location technology where the phone itself calculates its position using multiple sources combined.UE capability (LPP supported, A-GNSS, DBH, NR-ECID measurement reporting) and IMS emergency registration.
gNB / FemtoProvides NR-ECID measurements (Cell-ID + Rx-Tx, Angle-of-Arrival, RSRP/RSRQ) to the LMF over NRPPa; transmits PRS when configured.NR-ECID enabled on the cell, NRPPa procedure run cleanly, PCI / NRCI / TAC / PLMN match what the GMLC has whitelisted, Z-axis source plumbed in.
AMFRoutes the location request between the gNB and the LMF using the Namf_Location service; relays LPP and NRPPa containers.That the AMF is correctly issuing Namf_Location_ProvidePositioningInfo on an emergency call, and that the LMF reachability / selection logic in the AMF is intact.
LMF (Location Management Function)The brains — selects the positioning method (NR-ECID / GNSS / DBH), runs the calculation, applies the uncertainty rule and returns a fix.Method-selection policy, cell-radius / CID-uncertainty threshold (the case study above), NRPPa & LPP exchanges, Z-axis assembly, multi-LMF redundancy so a single upgrade window doesn’t kill E911.
GMLC (Gateway Mobile Location Centre)Operator-side gateway to LCS clients (PSAP, VAS, MTLR clients). Authenticates location requests and forwards the fix outbound.That the femto’s NRCI + TAC + PLMN are whitelisted at the GMLC, MTLR routing, and that the GMLC has all serving LMFs configured.
NRPPa (TS 38.455)The control protocol between LMF and gNB used to request and return positioning measurements (Rx-Tx, AoA, ECID, PRS measurements).Procedure success in the trace (Measurement Request / Response, Information Exchange), and that the gNB actually returns the requested IEs.
LPP / LPPa (TS 37.355 / 36.355)Positioning protocol between the LMF and the UE (LPP) and between the LMF and the eNB on EPS legs (LPPa) — carries assistance data and measurement reports end-to-end.LPP capability transfer, GNSS assistance data delivery (tells the UE which satellites to use and the data it needs to lock to them faster) and PRS configuration (tells the UE which downlink signal to use as the Positioning Reference Signal), and that LPP rides transparently through AMF / gNB.
NR-ECID (Enhanced Cell-ID)Network-based location method — the LMF derives the UE position from gNB-reported measurements (Cell-ID, Rx-Tx time difference, AoA, RSRP) collected via NRPPa.Cell is NR-ECID-capable, NRPPa measurements return real values, and the calculated uncertainty beats the configured CID radius (the 15 m policy case earlier on this page).
GNSS / A-GNSSUE-based location method — satellite-derived fix on the UE, optionally helped by network-supplied assistance data / PRS configurations over LPP.Assistance-data delivery via LPP, and graceful fallback to NR-ECID / DBH when the UE has no sky view (indoor femto).
DBH (Device-Based Hybrid)UE-side location method — the UE fuses GNSS + Wi-Fi + cellular + on-board sensors (barometric pressure) on the device to compute a complete 3D fix (lat / long / altitude). Used especially indoors where pure GNSS is unreliable, so it pairs naturally with the femto.DBH support on the UE, that the device returns a valid hybrid fix, and that the LMF accepts and forwards the DBH fix to the GMLC.
Z-axis (vertical altitude)The altitude component of the fix required by the FCC vertical-location rule. All three location methods above must supply Z-axis — NR-ECID via gNB-side altitude assembly, GNSS via the satellite fix’s altitude, and DBH from the UE’s barometric stack.That whichever method is selected, the returned location object carries lat + long + Z-axis, and that the Z value is passed end-to-end through the LMF → GMLC → EGIS → PSAP.
PSAPThe receiving end — the Public Safety Answering Point on the emergency-services IP network. Consumes the location object delivered via the GMLC.End-to-end delivery of the location object with the call, format alignment, and that the call is routed to the right PSAP even on a mismatched PLMN.
EGIS (Emergency Geographic Information Service)The operator’s E911 GIS layer that maps the location object to the right PSAP / response zone and supplements the routing path with mapping data.That the location object from the GMLC reaches EGIS and that EGIS routes the emergency call to the expected PSAP for the femto’s authorised location.
MSAG (Master Street Address Guide)The authoritative street-address database used by E911 routing — civic locations are validated against MSAG so the right PSAP and response zone are selected.That the femto’s civic address is MSAG-valid at provisioning time, so location objects translate into a deliverable street address at the PSAP end.
Spectrum Spatial (operator GIS platform)The operator’s GIS / mapping platform used to provision and visualise the femto’s authorised location footprint, BMC zones and emergency routing.Femto-cell location object & allowed-area polygon match what was provisioned, and the 20 m geo-lock check at bring-up agrees with the Spectrum Spatial record.
BMC (Broadcast / BMC component)The femto’s BMC entry — provisioned alongside LMF / GMLC during ZTP so the femto cell is recognised by the warning / broadcast ecosystem.BMC entry exists for the cell at NMS bring-up, ZTP file upload responded to with the BMC piece, and the femto reaches BMC under the permanent tunnels.
IMS / E-CSCFEmergency call control on the IMS side — routes the SIP INVITE to the correct PSAP based on the location.Emergency IMS registration, P-CSCF / E-CSCF reachability for the femto, and that VoNR↔RTT upgrades / downgrades work mid-call.
CBC / CBCF (CMAS / WEA)Cell Broadcast Centre / Function — pushes public warning messages (CMAS/WEA, Presidential, AMBER, Severe…) to the RAN, which broadcasts them in SIB-carried Warning Messages.Send / Replace / Update / Cancel handling, PWS failure & restart indications, and SIB scheduling alignment (the core-dump fix earlier on this page).
UDM / AUSFSubscription and authentication — including LCS (Location Services) authorization for non-emergency MTLR flows. LCS authorization answers the question: “is this person or application allowed to request this subscriber’s location?”That LCS privacy & authorization don’t block legitimate MTLRs, and that emergency calls bypass authorization where required by regulation.
