QoS · WPS, Slicing & Pre-emption

WPS & Network Slicing

WPS (Wireless Priority Service) and network slicing let a 5G SA network guarantee that priority traffic — emergency responders, government users — keeps working when the cell is congested. The behaviour is governed by per-flow QoS parameters (5QI, ARP, GBR/MBR) and by pre-emption: a priority UE can take resources from a normal UE, but never the reverse. I validate that this holds under load, and that slices stay isolated.

Pre-emption under congestion — how it should behave

💡I verified each rule by trace: WPS UEs are not pre-emptable; normal UEs are pre-empted by ARP to admit incoming WPS UEs; under max load normal UEs get rejected via cell temporary barring + access class barring on SIB1, and re-attach when resources free up. During handover with no free resource, the normal UE fails while the WPS UE succeeds.

Case study — the PCI/PVI gap that dropped live 911 calls

Symptom
Ongoing emergency (911) calls were being dropped during congestion — exactly the calls that must never drop.
Root cause
Pre-emption was configured ARP-priority-only. The emergency bearer had a high ARP priority value but its PCI/PVI flags were not set correctly (effectively pre-emptable), so the scheduler could victimise it when admitting other traffic.
Fix
Switched pre-emption to evaluate ARP + PCI + PVI together. With PCI=true / PVI=false applied to the priority bearer, an in-progress 911 call became non-pre-emptable and no longer dropped under congestion.

Why it matters: ARP priority alone is not enough — the pre-emption flags are what actually protect a bearer. This is a life-safety-critical distinction, and catching it in the lab is the entire point of pre-launch validation.

Case study — WPS dynamic ARP uplift (the *272 behaviour)

WPS users invoke priority by dialling *272 + the destination number. I validated a subtle, correct behaviour: when a WPS user calls a regular user (on 5QI 6), the network dynamically raises the regular user's ARP for the duration of that call — so the whole priority conversation is protected, not just the WPS leg. I confirmed by trace that the regular UE's ARP rose while on a call with the WPS UE, and returned afterward. I separately validated HPA (High Priority Access): with the SIM provisioned ACC = 11 and MPS off, the UE signals High Priority Access as its RRC establishment cause, mapping to ARP 4 / 5QI 128 / PCI=true / PVI=false.

Case study — WPS + Slicing bring-up failures

SIM can't see 5QI 128

WPS+Slicing configured but the SIM never saw 5QI 128. Root cause was twofold: SOC codes not correctly provisioned at the SIM and slicing not configured at the femto. Fixed both ends ⇒ 5QI 128 appeared.

Voice/data stalled on slice

Voice and data stalled on WPS+Slicing because the latency parameter wasn't configured and the default slice wasn't configured. Adding both restored service — a reminder that the default slice underpins everything.

Slice isolation & resource reservation

I validated three slices (default, 7000, 7001) for correct isolation and admission, and confirmed the three reservation modes behave per design:

Reservation modeExpected behaviour I verified
ExclusiveResources reserved for one slice cannot be used by another, even when idle.
PriorityHigher-priority slice gets preference when resources are shared.

Throughput prioritization held the intended order under congestion: Regular < WPS < Slice UE, with PRB assignment and admission control respecting WPS/HPA priority, and multi-slice support keeping ≥2 slices isolated simultaneously.

Field notes & common pitfalls

  • ARP priority ≠ protection. PCI/PVI flags decide pre-emption; a high-priority bearer with PVI=true can still be dropped.
  • WPS protection is dynamic. A regular user on a call with a WPS user inherits raised ARP for that call.
  • Default slice is foundational. Missing default-slice or latency config stalls voice/data even when the priority slice looks configured.
  • Provisioning is two-sided. WPS/5QI 128 needs both SIM (SOC/ACC) and femto (slicing) config — one side alone fails silently.
